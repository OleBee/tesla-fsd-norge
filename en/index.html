    European Commission prepares the next step for Tesla Assisted Driving – in case the EU says yes

    The European Commission has prepared for what happens next if the EU says yes to Tesla Assisted Driving (TAD), formerly FSD Supervised. That is clear from a slide from the 1 July meeting of the Commission’s Motor Vehicles Working Group (MVWG). A yellow box reads: “Preparation of Art. 40 (in case Art 39 of Tesla enters into force)”. The documents themselves are not new. The slide is from July, and the minutes of the meeting are dated 15 September. The German X account TADWatcherDE reported on the documents on Saturday evening. We have obtained them ourselves from CIRCABC, the Commission’s document archive. “In case” means contingency planning, not a promise. The presentation was produced by the Commission’s Joint Research Centre (JRC) and the Directorate-General for Internal Market and Industry (DG GROW). It carries a caveat that the views are preliminary and not the Commission’s final position. Article 40 of the EU type-approval regulation, (EU) 2018/858, sets out what happens after a yes. Once the Commission has granted an authorisation under Article 39, the exemption procedure for new technology, it must immediately start adapting the rules to the new technology. If the exemption concerns a UN regulation, the Commission must propose amendments to it. For TAD that is UN R171, the UN regulation on driver control assistance systems (DCAS). Once the rules have been amended, the restrictions in the authorisation fall away. In other words, the exemption becomes part of the ordinary rules. The slide also shows the priorities the Commission has gathered for phase 3 of the DCAS work, dated June 2026. High priority goes to system-initiated manoeuvres, where the car itself starts a manoeuvre such as a lane change, on motorways and motorway-like roads with hands off. The same applies to system-initiated manoeuvres in cities with hands on the wheel. Hands-off driving in cities has low or no priority. In the same presentation, however, the Commission lists hands-off driving on city streets as one of the points it must look at under Article 40. Today R171 allows it only on motorways. The question the Commission asks is how it affects the driver’s attention.

    Why it matters: The slide shows that the Commission was planning for a yes as early as the summer. It is not a decision. First the EU countries in TCMV, the EU’s Technical Committee on Motor Vehicles, must vote on whether the approval from RDW, the Dutch approval authority, should apply across the EU. The next chance is the December meeting. The date has not been set, and it is not known whether there will be a vote. In the minutes, the Netherlands describes how RDW assessed the system: data, studies and test reports, checks with simulation tools, on a test track and on public roads. The result was a provisional approval, because the system showed a level of safety as high as the UN regulation requires. The minutes refer to a Level 2 driver assistance system and do not name Tesla. The slide does. Switzerland asked how the system is monitored in use. The Netherlands replied that the results are shared with the countries that accept the approval. The road safety organisation ETSC (European Transport Safety Council) was concerned about the process. ETSC asked the MVWG subgroup on automated and connected vehicles to discuss the future of this type of driver assistance. The answer is in the same minutes: the approval is based on data, simulation and testing on track and road, and experience from use is shared with the countries. In the presentation the JRC also refers to its own test-track trials. There, the probability of a collision in critical situations was substantially lower with driver assistance than with manual driving. The trials concerned similar Level 2 systems, not TAD specifically. For Norway, Article 40 is good news in the long run. Regulation 2018/858 applies as Norwegian law through section 3-1 of the vehicle regulations. Statens vegvesen, the Norwegian Public Roads Administration, has said it will follow up under the EEA Agreement once the EU has decided. If the system becomes part of the ordinary EU rules, it is no longer an exemption Norway has to take a position on. That is our assessment. It is still telling that Brussels is planning step two while Norwegian owners are still waiting for step one. But the order follows the law: first the exemption, then permanent rules. TAD is still not approved for ordinary owners in Norway.

    RDW approves a new Tesla Assisted Driving update – hands on the wheel above 130 km/h

    The Dutch approval authority RDW has approved yet another update of Tesla Assisted Driving (TAD), formerly FSD Supervised. RDW announced this in a news release on 16 November, barely two weeks after the previous round of changes. The system warns earlier when the driver is not paying attention, and it can issue a “full strike”, a warning that counts in full, if the driver is inattentive in the middle of a manoeuvre. Above 130 km/h the driver must keep their hands on the wheel. The speed display also becomes clearer. When the system uses the variable speed feature Contextual Max Speed, the driver is notified, and the speed indicator pulses when the system avoids slowing down. If the driver wants a higher top speed than the system suggests, it must be confirmed with the scroll wheel on the steering wheel. If the car is actually driving faster, the speed limit sign on the screen flashes red, and the current limit is shown next to the speed. On top of this come general improvements that RDW does not describe further. The update applies to cars with hardware version 4.0 (HW4) or newer and arrives over the air. Tesla decides when.

    Why it matters: RDW tested the changes both on a test track and on ordinary roads and found no negative or unintended effects. The update is an extension of the Dutch type-approval under Article 39 of the EU framework regulation 2018/858, the exemption procedure for new technology. Finland’s Traficom wrote on 11 November that Tesla has been willing to make the changes member states have asked for. This is the second round in under two weeks, and it is a step in the right direction before TCMV, the EU’s Technical Committee on Motor Vehicles, takes a decision. The next chance of a yes is in December, but the meeting date has not been set. Eleven EU countries have said yes. In Norway TAD is still not approved for ordinary owners, and Statens vegvesen, the Norwegian Public Roads Administration, is waiting for the EU.
