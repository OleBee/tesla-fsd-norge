How we make the estimates

Every country has a date, but the dates are not equally strong. An estimate is based on a known decision. A modelled estimate is calculated with the model below, and every assumption is listed. None of the dates are promises from Tesla or the authorities, and we never invent facts.

Estimate based on sources

  1. Launched: The date is the first documented rollout to customers, announced by Tesla or observed by owners. If it is missing, we show the approval date and say so.
  2. Approved, rollout soon: The approval date plus the typical wait from approval to first rollout in the countries that already have FSD Supervised. Sometimes we show only the approval date and note that Tesla has not given a date.

Modelled estimate – five steps

  1. Step 1 – open national case: We take the date of the first public signal about the case and add the wait we have seen from such a signal to a decision. Current basis (days from signal to decision): Belgium 29, Czechia 103, Finland 140, Slovenia 58, Croatia 19, Latvia 35. The median is 46.5 days, rounded up to 47. The longest wait is 140 days. Lithuania, Estonia and Denmark are not included because we have not found a dated public signal from the authorities before the decision. The Netherlands is not included because RDW, the Dutch type-approval authority, granted the approval itself. If the median has passed without a decision, we use the longest wait. If that has passed too, the country moves to step 2. Finally, we add the wait until rollout.
  2. Step 2 – the EU scenario: The Tesla case is on the agenda of the EU’s Technical Committee on Motor Vehicles (TCMV) on 15 December 2026, with a possible vote. We assume a yes, but that is an assumption. We then add the time it took from a TCMV yes until the EU approval of Ford’s BlueCruise was in place: 132 days (TCMV said yes in the week up to 20 March 2024; Ford announced the EU approval on 30 July 2024). With the wait until rollout, that gives 28 April 2027.
  3. Step 3 – Norway and the EEA: Here we show a range, not a single date. Norway is sorted by the earliest date.
    At the earliest 30 April 2027: This assumes that Norway follows the EU shortly after the decision. That is possible. The EU type-approval regulation applies as a Norwegian regulation (vehicle regulations § 3-1), and Statens vegvesen writes that it will “handle and continue the process in line with the obligations of the EEA Agreement” once the EU decision is in place (vegvesen.no, our translation). With the UN regulation for such driver assistance systems, R171, things moved quickly: it entered into force on 22 September 2024 (UN), and according to Statens vegvesen such systems have been eligible for type-approval in Norway since September 2024. No source says how short a Norwegian lag will be. We therefore add 2 days after the EU scenario, the same as the median rollout wait. That is our assumption. Statens vegvesen has said it will review Tesla’s customer information, as it did with Ford, and that it cannot give a timeline (ITavisen).
    At the latest 10 August 2028: This assumes it goes as slowly as with Ford’s BlueCruise. From the EU approval on 30 July 2024, it took 470 days until Statens vegvesen and the Ministry of Transport established on 12 November 2025 that it also applies in Norway. Iceland and Liechtenstein get the same range. For them, both dates are assumptions.
  4. Step 4 – no known case: The EU scenario plus 30 days. The margin is an assumption.
  5. Step 5 – outside the EU and the EEA: Switzerland and the United Kingdom: the EU scenario plus 180 days. The margin is an assumption; we have no observed precedent.

Certainty: high means the date is documented by an authority, Tesla or several independent sources. Medium means the date rests on one source, owner posts or a simple calculation from a known decision. Low means the date comes from the model. All modelled estimates have low certainty.

We check the sources and rerun the model every day. If we get a confirmed date for the TCMV vote, a new decision or a new signal, the dates move – and you will see it under “last updated”.

TAD Norge – unofficial status site for Tesla FSD Supervised in Norway.
TAD stands for Tesla Assisted Driving, the name Tesla now uses on its European websites for the system previously called FSD Supervised. New name, same system.
